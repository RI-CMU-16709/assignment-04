Section 1 diagnosis: what is wrong with drawer_scene_bad.xml
===========================================================

Five defects are marked with DEFECT n comments in the file. The comments tell
you WHERE to look, not what is wrong or how to fix it. For each one you must
report, in one or two sentences each:

  (a) the symptom you can observe in the viewer or in a short simulation,
  (b) the physical or numerical cause,
  (c) the change you made and why that value.

Save the repaired model as scene/drawer_scene.xml. Keep the same task: the same
robot, the same cabinet and drawer geometry, the same handle site, the same weld,
and the same keyframe. Do not delete the drawer, weld it shut, disable gravity,
or drop the timestep below 0.0005.

The five areas
--------------

DEFECT 1  <option>
    Look at the integration settings. Two of the three attributes there are
    unsuitable for a contact-rich scene with stiff position actuators.
    Hint: what stability limit does an explicit integrator have, and how does
    it scale with the timestep?

DEFECT 2  cabinet_shell geom
    Compare the shell's extent with where the drawer front sits, then ask
    whether those two geoms should be able to collide at all. inspect_scene.py
    prints the overlap in millimetres and whether the pair is collidable.

DEFECT 3  drawer_slide joint
    Two attributes are missing or zero. One of them lets the drawer leave the
    cabinet entirely; the other means nothing ever dissipates the energy you
    put into it.

DEFECT 4  drawer <inertial>
    The mass on this element is reasonable for a wooden drawer, and the mass
    check in inspect_scene.py passes. Look at the rest of the element.

    A body has two inertial properties, not one. Compare the second one against
    the same property of the Panda links that have to move this drawer, and ask
    what a ratio of that size does to the constraint solver.

    Hint: you can see this one. In the viewer's Rendering panel, enable the
    "Inertia" model element - MuJoCo then draws the equivalent inertia box of
    every body. Compare the drawer's box with the arm's.

DEFECT 5  drawer_handle geoms
    The drawer has a bracket handle built from two geoms. Print both of them
    with their sizes AND their contact attributes before you decide which one
    the gripper actually meets. The viewer shows you shape; it does not show
    you what is collidable.

    For the geom that does collide: the tool closes on the handle at roughly
    0.2 to 0.5 m/s during the approach phase. Work out how far a body travels
    in one timestep at that speed and compare it with the size of that geom.

    Then think about persistence rather than a single instant. Contact is
    re-detected from scratch every step, and the solver needs a contact that
    lives for many consecutive steps to build up any force. Count how many
    steps the gripper and the handle can possibly overlap on one pass. A
    handful of steps is not a grasp - it is a tap, and the weld has nothing to
    hold on to when it activates.

Self-check
----------

    python section1/inspect_scene.py

That script re-runs the checks and prints PASS/FAIL for each of the five, plus
a 10-second stability run and a timestep-convergence comparison. You need 5/5
PASS and both dynamic tests green before starting Section 2 - a controller
tuned against a broken model teaches you nothing about control.

Reporting a passing check without explaining the cause earns no marks. The
grading looks at your explanation, not only at the diff.
